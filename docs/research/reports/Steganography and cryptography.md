# Detect hidden data first, embed nothing yet

**Build defensive detection first and leave embedding and encryption for a separate maintainer decision.** The best-evidenced threat to MarkCopy's users is not covert messaging but invisible Unicode and appended payloads in files they preview: Unicode variation selectors hid JavaScript in the GlassWorm VS Code extension campaign (about 35,800 installs in the first wave, waves continuing into 2026) ([Koi/Palo Alto](https://www.paloaltonetworks.com/blog/security-operations/glassworm-first-self-propagating-worm-using-invisible-code-hits-openvsx-marketplace/)), Unicode Tags carry prompt injection against coding agents ([Pillar Security](https://pillar.security/blog/new-vulnerability-in-github-copilot-and-cursor-how-hackers-can-weaponize-code-agents)), and Microsoft reported tag-character phishing evasion in September 2026 ([Microsoft](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/security/blog/2026/09/03/ascii-smuggling/)). MarkCopy today flags none of this: its only invisible-character handling is [src/binary/text.ts](../../../src/binary/text.ts), which strips bidi and control characters from text pulled out of binaries and is not applied to Markdown, CSV or Office text. The recommended order is (1) an invisible-Unicode scanner for text formats, (2) a "hidden data" structure inspector for images, PDF and zip-based files on the existing worker pipeline, (3) an Office hidden-content report, (4) cheap PE overlay and zip trailer follow-ups, then (5) a provenance (C2PA) panel. Statistical pixel steganalysis should not ship as an automatic check, and embed/encrypt features (an encrypted-payload hider, per-recipient fingerprinting, seed-phrase hiding) all conflict with stated stances and need an explicit maintainer decision; I recommend against the seed-phrase one outright. Evidence quality is uneven: much of the byte-level format detail and the audio, video and structural-hiding material is background knowledge not re-verified, and this report marks where that matters.

## How strong the evidence is

The six research notes differ sharply in rigor, and the recommendations lean on the strongest parts. Well-sourced: the abuse timeline for invisible Unicode (Trojan Source 2021, os-info-checker-es6 in March 2025, GlassWorm from October 2025, Microsoft's September 2026 phishing report), the Argon2id, scrypt and AEAD parameter guidance, the MarkCopy architecture facts (read directly from the repository at commit 9630bc3), and npm library versions (read from the registry on 2026-11-26). Search-snippet only: most steganalysis error rates (for example SRM error of 0.13 to 0.18 on BOSSbase at 0.4 bpp against adaptive schemes, aggregated across papers with differing setups ([arXiv 1612.08882](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1612.08882))), the cryptocurrency campaign details, and the C2PA adoption analysis, which comes from a secondary source (SoftwareSeni) the note itself says to verify. Background knowledge, explicitly not re-verified: all PNG, JPEG, GIF, BMP, SVG, WAV, MP3, MP4, PDF-scan and OOXML detection rules in the structural note (only the specs it quotes directly are sourced), the whole audio and video steganography topic, LSB and HUGO/WOW/HILL detectability, and the claim that no JS port of syndrome-trellis codes exists (a negative result from limited searching). Where a claim below rests on that background material, it is marked "(background)".

Two gaps matter for decisions rather than details. No source establishes whether `crypto.subtle` works in VS Code webviews across the supported range (circumstantial evidence says VS Code's own webview host needs it, but no official statement was found), which is why the crypto design below runs in the extension host. And no source covers national laws on steganography, Marketplace or Open VSX policy on dual-use tools, or journalist and activist legal risk, so the legal section is thin by necessity.

## The state of the art: cost maps and trellis codes, paired with encryption that makes bits random

Modern secure image steganography is a two-part recipe: a distortion cost map that says which pixels or DCT coefficients are cheap to change, and syndrome-trellis coding (STC) that embeds the payload while minimizing total cost. S-UNIWARD is the spatial instance and J-UNIWARD the JPEG one ([arXiv 2305.19776](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.19776)). A 2023 paper found an off-by-one error in J-UNIWARD's published Matlab and C++ reference code, which matters to anyone porting it to TypeScript. The older F5 was broken in 2002 through the rise in zero coefficients its shrinkage causes, and nsF5 (wet paper codes) is still detectable with modern features ([Binghamton nsF5](https://dde.binghamton.edu/download/nsf5simulator)). Capacity follows the square-root law: detectability stays constant only if payload grows with the square root of the number of pixels ([Vlasov thesis](https://www.cs.ox.ac.uk/people/andrew.ker/docs/vlasov-empirical-validation-square-root-law.pdf)), so hiding in a small image is inherently limited. Robust embedding that survives recompression remains weak: one STC plus J-UNIWARD scheme built to survive recompression was reported as highly detectable, with 5% detection error at quality factor 75 ([arXiv 2211.04750](https://arxiv.org/html/2211.04750v2)). Social platforms recompress images and reportedly strip metadata, so covert channels through them are unreliable; the actual platform quantization settings were not found.

The cryptographic layer is comparatively settled. Encrypting first makes the embedded bits uniformly random, which is the assumption that LSB and STC security analyses make, and the design guidance that emerged from the research is a no-header blob: `salt || nonce || AEAD(length || data || padding)`, with all parameters fixed by format version rather than stored in plaintext (design judgment, not a sourced standard). For the key derivation, RFC 9106's second recommended Argon2id setting is t=3, p=4, 64 MiB with a 128-bit salt ([RFC 9106](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9106.html#section-4)); OWASP's floor is 19 MiB, t=2, p=1 ([OWASP](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Password_Storage_Cheat_Sheet.html)). Node's native `crypto.argon2` only arrived in v24.7.0, so the Node 20 extension host does not have it ([Node docs](https://nodejs.org/api/crypto.html)); `hash-wasm` offers WASM Argon2 at about 11 kB gzipped ([hash-wasm](https://github.com/Daninet/hash-wasm)) but its licence was not confirmed, while `@noble/hashes` has pure-JS Argon2 that its own README calls slow and that its audit excluded ([noble-hashes](https://github.com/paulmillr/noble-hashes)). For the cipher, `@noble/ciphers` recommends XChaCha20-Poly1305 because its 24-byte nonce is safe to pick at random, and warns that random 96-bit AES-GCM nonces carry collision risk ([noble-ciphers](https://github.com/paulmillr/noble-ciphers)). Node's built-in `scrypt` and AES-256-GCM would avoid any new dependency, at the cost of the nonce caveat. Public-key stego is a research risk: hiding an ephemeral key needs Elligator2 encoding, which `noble-curves` does not provide, and age's headers are plaintext and identifiable by design ([age spec](https://github.com/C2SP/C2SP/blob/main/age.md)). Passphrase mode is the only defensible v1.

Prior tools show what to avoid. Steghide's embedding RNG has only 2^32 seeds (CVE-2021-27211), which lets Stegseek check all of rockyou.txt in under two seconds and recover unencrypted payloads without any passphrase ([Stegseek](https://github.com/RickdeJager/stegseek), [NVD](https://nvd.nist.gov/vuln/detail/CVE-2021-27211)). StegCloak, the one text-stego tool with traction, makes encryption optional and warns that its zero-width output is itself detectable ([StegCloak](https://github.com/KuroLabs/stegcloak)). Reusing a key across images lets attackers locate payloads ([arXiv 1511.04855](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1511.04855)). Because an attacker can test passphrase guesses offline against any suspect image at Argon2 cost, with the AEAD tag as the oracle, there is no rate limiting to lean on. The product gap is real but narrow: the notes found no maintained, authenticated-encryption stego tool with a good interface (Steghide is read-only and seeking maintainers ([GitHub](https://github.com/StefanoDeVuono/steghide))), but that absence mostly reflects thin demand, since stegcloak shows about 3.8k stars against hundreds of weekly downloads, a curiosity and CTF profile.

Text channels are the exception to "hard to do well": Unicode Tags (U+E0000 to U+E007F) and variation selectors give one invisible code point per byte with trivial code ([Paul Butler](https://paulbutler.org/2025/smuggling-arbitrary-data-through-an-emoji/)), but they are no longer secret, because defenders now strip or normalize them ([Microsoft](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/security/blog/2026/09/03/ascii-smuggling/)), and an encrypted blob in them still shows up as a long run of invisible characters. LLM-based generative steganography has provably secure constructions such as Meteor ([ePrint 2021/686](https://eprint.iacr.org/2021/686)) and minimum entropy coupling ([arXiv 2210.14889](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.14889)), though security claims for interval-based schemes are contested ([arXiv 2604.08052](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.08052)). All of them need explicit next-token distributions and exactly shared numerics, so they are out of reach for an extension. Audio and video steganography was not researched with sources; (background) WAV LSB is easy and fragile to any lossy codec, and MP3 or video embedding needs encoder-level access.

## Defenders win on structure, not statistics

Steganalysis splits into two very different tools. Statistical detectors (chi-square, RS, sample pairs) target LSB replacement and need decoded pixels. StegExpose fuses them with a single 0.2 threshold and publishes no per-detector false-positive rates ([StegExpose](https://github.com/b3dk7/StegExpose)). LSB matching (plus or minus one) "has proved much harder to detect than LSB replacement" ([Ker, SPIE 5681](https://medicalimaging.spiedigitallibrary.org/conference-proceedings-of-spie/5681/0000/Resampling-and-the-detection-of-LSB-matching-in-color-bitmaps/10.1117/12.586622.full)), and adaptive schemes were built to defeat classical tests. Rich-model and CNN detectors do better in the lab (one report puts a CNN at 7.4% error against S-UNIWARD at 0.4 bpp versus about 20% for rich models ([arXiv 1605.07946](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1605.07946))) but a 2020 review says even the best deep models are prone to cover-source mismatch ([Giboulot et al.](https://eurasip.org/Proceedings/Eusipco/Eusipco2022/pdfs/0001032.pdf)), and a previewer receives exactly that condition: arbitrary phone photos, screenshots and re-saved web images. No browser-sized steganalysis model turned up. My inference, flagged as unmeasured, is that smooth synthetic content such as screenshots and line art will inflate false positives for the classical tests, and a "clean" verdict would be misleading because no cheap test catches adaptive embedding. The sound conclusion is that statistical steganalysis cannot back a claim of the form MarkCopy is willing to print.

Structural checks are different: they are deterministic, cheap, and produce facts with offsets. The PNG third edition says no chunks or other content may follow IEND, defines the ancillary, private and safe-to-copy case bits, and requires consecutive IDAT chunks, so a linear chunk walk with CRC checks yields clean findings ([W3C PNG 3](https://www.w3.org/TR/png-3/)). Appended payloads are documented in the wild: PNG metadata holding a web-shell command, a JPG with reversed Base64 PE appended, XWorm "PNGs" that are Base64 plus AES blobs, and Worok hiding payloads in PNG pixel LSBs ([OPSWAT](https://www.opswat.com/docs/filescan/datasheet/steganography-analysis), [Cybersecurity News](https://cybersecuritynews.com/steganography-attacks-xworm-in-pngs/amp/), [ESET](https://www.welivesecurity.com/2022/09/06/worok-big-picture/); vendor sources, dates unclear). ZIP-concatenation tricks have evaded antivirus, with only 16 of 34 engines detecting one sample ([BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/hackers-now-use-zip-file-concatenation-to-evade-detection/)). PDF incremental updates are legal and standard-compliant, which is why shadow attacks worked against 16 of 29 tested viewers ([NDSS 2021](https://ndss-symposium.org/ndss-paper/shadow-attacks-hiding-and-replacing-content-in-signed-pdfs)); counting `%%EOF` markers counts revisions ([SANS ISC](https://isc.sans.edu/diary/25904)). Office files hide content legitimately through `hidden` and `veryHidden` sheet states, `w:vanish` runs and slides with `show="0"` ([Open XML SDK](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dotnet/api/documentformat.openxml.spreadsheet.sheetstatevalues)).

The false-positive risk lives in legitimate trailers and private chunks, and it is why findings must be worded as facts, not verdicts. Google motion photos append a video after the JPEG and require it to be the last item in the file ([Android Developers](https://developer.android.com/media/platform/motion-photo-format)); Samsung uses an undocumented trailer; MPF and gain-map secondary images start with their own JPEG header; C2PA manifests will make large `caBX` PNG chunks common; Apple CgBI PNGs are non-conforming by design; OCR PDFs carry invisible text; and Android APKs and self-extractors legitimately have gaps (all background except the motion-photo spec). The structural note's cheap rule is to classify a trailer as "declared" when container XMP or the MPF index accounts for the exact bytes, and "undeclared" otherwise.

The editor-side gap is real but narrower than it first looks. VS Code offers `editor.unicodeHighlight.invisibleCharacters` (users often disable it) and GitHub shows a hidden-Unicode banner since May 2025 ([GitHub changelog](https://github.blog/changelog/2025-05-01-github-now-provides-a-warning-about-hidden-unicode-text)), yet GlassWorm persisted for months afterward, and nothing covers rendered Markdown, images, PDFs, Office files or the path an AI agent reads. The market signal is modest: the Gremlins tracker extension reported 100,000 installs in 2020 ([author note](https://nicolas-hoizey.com/notes/2020/10/05/1/)), no current counts were found, and existing extensions mostly highlight or strip. The unserved niche is "decode and explain" inside the preview.

## Cryptocurrency, provenance and the limits of legitimate use

The cryptocurrency picture is lopsided toward attackers. Hiding a seed phrase in an image is widely written up, and the critiques are persuasive: steganography is not encryption, a lossy re-save can destroy the payload, it adds a second secret to lose, and heirs may not know the toolchain (search synthesis; Trezor and Ledger guidance was not retrieved, and SLIP-39 was not researched). The cautionary precedent is brain wallets, where 884 wallets worth about $100K were found and all but 21 were drained, usually within 24 hours, because the key was an unsalted single SHA-256 of a human passphrase ([UCL](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/10154816/1/vasekfc16.pdf)). On the offensive side, Google reported North Korea-linked UNC5342 using smart contracts on Ethereum and BNB Smart Chain as resilient command-and-control (EtherHiding) ([Google Cloud](https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/threat-intelligence/dprk-adopts-etherhiding)), GlassWorm used Solana for C2 and targeted 49 crypto extensions, and Bitcoin Core v30 raised the default OP_RETURN relay limit from about 80 bytes to 100,000 bytes ([Forklog](https://forklog.com/en/bitcoin-core-developers-release-controversial-update/)). Stealer families (Vidar, PXA) hide Base64 in JPEG and PNG files and exfiltrate over Telegram; these are anecdotal incident reports, not aggregate counts. The implication for MarkCopy is that crypto-adjacent value lies in detecting payloads in files a developer opens, not in a "hide your seed" feature.

Provenance is the large legitimate market. C2PA published spec 2.3 in February 2026 with a claimed 6,000-plus members and affiliates, TikTok joined its steering committee in July, and Google is bringing verification to Gemini, Search and Chrome ([C2PA](https://c2pa.org/the-c2pa-launches-content-credentials-2-3-and-celebrates-5-years-of-impact-across-the-digital-ecosystem/), [CAI](https://contentauthenticity.org/blog/the-state-of-content-authenticity-in-2026)). Durability is the weakness: C2PA is signed metadata that re-encoding and platform uploads strip, so the Content Authenticity Initiative pairs it with Adobe's MIT-licensed TrustMark watermark and a cloud lookup as "soft binding" ([CAI durable credentials](https://opensource.contentauthenticity.org/docs/durable-cr/), [TrustMark](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/adobe/trustmark/main/README.md)). One secondary analysis reports a wide gap between claimed and certified conformance (verify before quoting) ([SoftwareSeni](https://www.softwareseni.com/c2pa-adoption-in-2026-hardware-platforms-and-verification-reality)). EU AI Act Article 50 now drives machine-readable marking: the Code of Practice on AI-generated content was published 10 June 2026, deployer obligations apply from 2 August 2026, and a Digital Omnibus defers some marking duties to 2 December 2026, with sources disagreeing on the exact split and the code itself voluntary ([European Commission](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/code-practice-ai-generated-content), [Freshfields](https://www.freshfields.com/en/our-thinking/blogs/technology-quotient/eu-ai-act-unpacked-33-the-final-code-of-practice-on-transparency-of-ai-generate-102n4yx)). Text watermarking is probabilistic: SynthID-Text is open source but weak on short or heavily rewritten text and does not stop motivated adversaries ([Google AI docs](https://ai.google.dev/responsible/docs/safeguards/synthid)). Per-recipient zero-width fingerprinting (canary traps) is established but fragile against retyping or normalization ([Hackaday](https://hackaday.com/2018/04/15/hide-secret-messages-in-plain-sight-with-zero-width-characters)).

On law, the one sourced point is favorable: publicly available encryption source is generally outside the Export Administration Regulations, with a notification only for non-standard cryptography ([BIS](https://bis.gov/node/6731), [Linux Foundation guidance](https://bestpractices.linuxfoundation.org/export/non-standard-cryptography.html); sources run 2000 to 2011, so verify on the live eCFR). Using only platform primitives or well-known libraries would likely stay in "standard cryptography" territory. This is not legal advice, and nothing was found on national steganography law, marketplace dual-use policy or activist risk. Ethically, defensive framing (detect, inspect, verify, mark your own content) is low risk, whereas a polished embed-and-encrypt tool for a mainstream preview extension invites questions about misuse that the maintainer should weigh deliberately.

## What MarkCopy could build, ranked

MarkCopy's fit is unusually good for detection. It is read-only by design, its pages "state what the file establishes, and no more", and it already has a confirm-first worker pipeline for risky binaries: [src/fileInfoEditor.ts](../../../src/fileInfoEditor.ts) shows a confirmation page, [src/isolate.ts](../../../src/isolate.ts) runs [src/fileInfoWorker.ts](../../../src/fileInfoWorker.ts) with a 256 MB heap cap and 15 s timer, and [src/fileInfoTask.ts](../../../src/fileInfoTask.ts) dispatches on a closed `kind` union (`'exe' | 'package'`). Reusable parts include the ranged reader in [src/binary/reader.ts](../../../src/binary/reader.ts), `printable` and `clip` in [src/binary/text.ts](../../../src/binary/text.ts), `bound()` in [src/pkg/index.ts](../../../src/pkg/index.ts), and the zip readers [src/pkg/zipRange.ts](../../../src/pkg/zipRange.ts) and [src/ooxml/zip.ts](../../../src/ooxml/zip.ts). Every new reader must also follow the project's rules: fixture builders, a Jazzer fuzz target with seeds, a page oracle, and documentation updates (see [docs/FUZZING-STRATEGY.md](../../FUZZING-STRATEGY.md) and [AGENTS.md](../../../AGENTS.md)). Detection needs no new dependency; the existing runtime set is small and the worker bundle is about 412 KB, so a hand-written reader adds kilobytes.

My recommended first build is rank 1, paired with the Markdown half of the preview's copy path. It is the smallest piece, addresses the best-documented attacks against exactly MarkCopy's audience (developers opening READMEs, agent rules files and skills), needs no dependency, and its false-positive profile is controllable through run-length and context rules. Rank 2 should follow as the substantive feature, with PNG, JPEG and zip first and PDF second, because those match the sourced malware cases. A note on a side effect worth documenting: Copy Image as PNG already re-encodes through a canvas, which drops appended data and most metadata, so a visible "this copy strips appended data and metadata" statement in [docs/COPY-MATRIX.md](../../COPY-MATRIX.md) is a nearly free, honest "sanitizing copy" feature (the repository note infers this; it was not tested).

If the maintainer wants any embed feature, the research supports exactly one design: passphrase mode only, mandatory authenticated encryption (no "no encryption" option), per-message random salt, Argon2id at RFC 9106's 64 MiB second setting (falling back to OWASP's 19 MiB floor if single-threaded WASM is too slow), HKDF subkeys for encryption and position permutation, random-looking layout with no magic header, Reed-Solomon over the ciphertext where the channel is lossy, host-side crypto, and a prominent warning that steganography is not encryption and that offline guessing cannot be rate-limited. The Reed-Solomon packages found are erasure-coding libraries, not error correctors, so a small RS(255,k) decoder would need writing (engineering judgment, unverified).

## Conclusion

The research reverses the obvious framing. "Steganography plus cryptography" sounds like a feature to embed, but the evidence says the field is a solved-in-theory, thin-in-demand space on the embedding side (old maintained-by-nobody tools, secure schemes needing STC and cost maps that no JS port covers, and a payload that is still detectable by statistics when done carelessly) while the live, growing, documented pressure is on the defensive side, where cheap deterministic structure and Unicode checks outperform any probabilistic detector and fit MarkCopy's "state what the file establishes" discipline. Provenance reading is the one other direction with regulatory momentum, but its value is capped by metadata stripping.

What would change this: a maintainer who wants an embed feature as a deliberate product bet should first resolve the scope conflicts in [IMAGE-DESIGN.md](../../../docs/IMAGE-DESIGN.md) and the read-only stance, and verify the open questions the notes left unanswered (whether `crypto.subtle` works in supported webviews, hash-wasm's licence, STC licensing, Marketplace dual-use policy). Before shipping rank 2, spot-check every "(background)" byte-level rule against the named specs (PNG 3 sections 11.3, ISO 14496-12, CIPA DC-007, GIF89a, ISO 32000-2, ECMA-376) and measure false-positive rates on real motion photos, C2PA-signed images and ordinary Office and PDF files, because the repository's own rule is to record measured results, not assumed coverage.
